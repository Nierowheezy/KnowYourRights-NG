Police have stopped my vehicle

What to do, your rights, and safety-first steps during a police traffic stop in Nigeria

Last reviewed: January 2025 Reviewed by: Legal Aid Council Source: Nigeria Police Act 2020, S. 38-40

Safety first

Do not physically resist. Stay calm, keep hands visible on the steering wheel, and comply with safety-related instructions. You can challenge the stop's legality later through proper channels.

Short answer

Police officers may legally stop vehicles for traffic violations, routine checks, or reasonable suspicion of crime. You must stop when signaled. You must produce a valid driver's license and vehicle registration on request. You have the right to ask why you were stopped and to note the officer's details. You do not have to consent to a search of your vehicle or phone, though officers may search without consent in certain circumstances (see Searches).

What the law says

Police powers to stop vehicles

Under the Nigeria Police Act 2020, Sections 38–40, police officers have the power to stop and search any vehicle where they have reasonable grounds to suspect it contains evidence of an offence, or for routine traffic checks. The Road Traffic Act and National Road Traffic Regulations also authorize stops for traffic enforcement.

Your obligations

  • Stop when signaled — Failure to stop is an offence.
  • Produce documents — You must show a valid driver's license and vehicle registration (proof of ownership) on request (Police Act 2020, S. 40; FRSC Act 2007).
  • Identify yourself — You must give your name and address if asked.

Your rights

  • Know the reason — You may ask why you were stopped.
  • Officer identification — You may ask for the officer's name, rank, and station.
  • Refuse consent to search — You can say "I do not consent to a search." This does not guarantee they won't search, but it preserves your rights.
  • Remain silent — Beyond identifying yourself and producing documents, you are not required to answer investigative questions.
  • Document the encounter — When safe, note time, location, officer details, vehicle plate, and what was said.

Important exceptions

  • Reasonable suspicion — If officers have reasonable grounds to suspect the vehicle contains evidence of a crime (e.g., weapons, stolen goods, drugs), they may search without consent (Police Act 2020, S. 39).
  • Checkpoints — At authorized roadblocks/checkpoints, broader search powers may apply.
  • Arrest — If you are arrested, your vehicle and person may be searched incident to arrest.
  • State variations — Some states have additional traffic laws (e.g., Lagos State Traffic Law). LASTMA and other state agencies have different powers than the Police.
  • Commercial vehicles — Different rules may apply to commercial drivers and vehicles.

Unresolved: The exact scope of "routine checks" without individualized suspicion is subject to legal debate. Courts have not definitively settled whether random stops without any stated reason are lawful under the 2020 Act.

What you can do now

  1. Pull over safely — Use your indicator, stop in a safe, well-lit place if possible.
  2. Stay in the vehicle — Unless asked to exit. Keep hands visible on the steering wheel.
  3. Be polite and calm — "Good afternoon, officer. How can I help?"
  4. Ask the reason — "May I know why I was stopped?"
  5. Produce documents — Hand over license and registration when requested.
  6. Note details — Mentally or physically record: time, location, officer name/badge, vehicle plate, agency.
  7. Do not consent to search — "I do not consent to a search of my vehicle." Say it calmly.
  8. If asked to exit — Comply for safety. Lock your door behind you if possible.

What to avoid

  • Do not argue, shout, or be confrontational.
  • Do not physically resist or attempt to drive away.
  • Do not offer money or valuables to "settle" the matter.
  • Do not hand over your phone unlocked unless legally compelled (see Phone Searches).
  • Do not sign any statement you haven't read and understood.

What to document later

When you are safe and the encounter is over, record:

  • Date, time, and exact location (landmarks, road name, GPS if possible)
  • Officer's name, rank, badge number, vehicle plate, agency (NPF, FRSC, LASTMA, etc.)
  • Reason given for the stop
  • What documents were requested and whether you produced them
  • Whether a search was conducted, with or without consent
  • Any money demanded or paid (get a receipt if you pay an official fine)
  • Names/contact of any witnesses
  • Photos of any damage, documents, or relevant scene details (when safe and lawful)

What to do afterward

  • If money was demanded: Report to the Police Complaints Response Unit (CRU) — npf.gov.ng/complaint or call 0805 700 0001 / 0805 700 0002.
  • If rights were violated: Contact the National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) — nhrc.gov.ng.
  • If vehicle was impounded: Request an official receipt and impoundment report. See Vehicle Impounded.
  • Legal help: Legal Aid Council of Nigeria (legalaidcouncil.gov.ng) or NGOs like Access to Justice (accesstojustice.org.ng).
