About KnowYourRights NG

Free, source-backed, safety-first guidance for roadside encounters in Nigeria

Our mission

People in Nigeria encounter police, FRSC, vehicle inspection officers, customs, and other authorities every day. During these encounters, most people don't know what the law permits, what an officer can legally request, how to respond safely, or where to get help afterward.

The relevant laws are spread across different sources, hard to search on a phone, and often written in dense legal language. People rely on hearsay, social media, or broad claims that leave out important exceptions.

KnowYourRights NG exists to change that. We provide a free, mobile-first, searchable guide to Nigerian rights and practical options during roadside enforcement encounters — organized by situation, written in plain English, and backed by verifiable legal sources.

What we do

  • Organize by situation — You don't need to know the name of a law. Search by what's happening: "police want my phone," "FRSC stopped me," "asked for money."
  • Explain in plain English — Rights, duties, and enforcement powers in language anyone can understand.
  • Link to sources — Every substantive claim links to the actual Act, regulation, section, or authoritative source.
  • Distinguish federal vs. state — Lagos traffic law ≠ federal law. We make the jurisdiction clear.
  • Explain exceptions — We don't present broad claims as universal rules. We tell you when the answer depends on specific facts.
  • Safety-first guidance — Practical, calm steps that prioritize your immediate safety over being legally "right."
  • Work offline — Core guides cache for offline access after first load.
  • Stay free — No accounts, no ads, no tracking, no paywalls.

Legal review process

Legal accuracy is our core product requirement. We do not publish unreviewed content as verified guidance.

Our content statuses

  • Draft — Initial research, not yet reviewed
  • Under review — With a Nigerian lawyer for verification
  • Lawyer-reviewed — Approved by a qualified Nigerian lawyer; sources verified
  • Needs revision — Legal change identified or error found
  • Retired — Superseded or no longer supported by current law

Review standards

  • Every scenario reviewed by a Nigerian lawyer before publication
  • Sources distinguished: primary law (Acts, Regulations) vs. secondary commentary
  • Review dates and reviewer identity displayed on each scenario
  • Re-review triggered by legislative changes, court judgments, or official procedure updates
  • Correction mechanism for users and legal reviewers

We do not use AI output as legal verification. We do not label content as lawyer-reviewed unless that review has actually occurred.

Scope & limitations

What this guide covers (MVP)

  • Common roadside encounters: stops, document requests, searches, payments, arrest, impoundment
  • Primary agencies: Nigeria Police Force, FRSC, LASTMA (Lagos example), Nigeria Customs Service
  • Federal law + selected state-specific procedures (Lagos as initial state example)
  • ~15–25 lawyer-reviewed scenarios in initial release

What this guide does NOT do

  • Not legal advice — This is general legal information, not individualized legal counsel
  • Not a lawyer — Does not create an attorney-client relationship
  • Not emergency services — Cannot intervene in active incidents; call 112/199 for emergencies
  • Not a complaint platform — We link to official channels; we don't accept or investigate complaints
  • Not guaranteed outcomes — Following guidance doesn't prevent arrest, fines, or seizure
  • Not exhaustive — Doesn't cover every agency, every state law, or every possible situation
  • Not a substitute for counsel — For your specific case, consult a qualified Nigerian lawyer

Privacy & security

  • No accounts required — Read the guide anonymously
  • No personal data collected — No names, phone numbers, locations, or incident reports by default
  • No tracking — No analytics, no third-party trackers, no cookies for advertising
  • Local search only — Search runs entirely on your device; queries never leave your phone
  • HTTPS everywhere — All connections encrypted
  • Offline-first — Works without internet after first visit

If we add optional feedback in the future, it will be anonymous, opt-in, and clearly explained.

Report an issue / Request a correction

Found something unclear, outdated, or incorrect? We want to know.

  • Legal content errors: Email legal@knowyourrights.ng with the scenario, the issue, and your source (if any)
  • Broken links: Email links@knowyourrights.ng with the page and link
  • Missing scenarios: Email suggest@knowyourrights.ng with the situation you couldn't find
  • General feedback: Use the feedback form (coming soon) or email hello@knowyourrights.ng

All submissions are reviewed before any legal content changes. We do not treat user-submitted claims as verified legal facts.

Source register

We maintain a register of all legal sources used in this guide. Key sources include:

  • Nigeria Police Act, 2020
  • Federal Road Safety Corps (Establishment) Act, 2007
  • National Road Traffic Regulations, 2012
  • Nigeria Customs Service Act, 2023
  • Lagos State Traffic Law, 2018 (and other state laws as covered)
  • 1999 Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria (as amended)
  • Administration of Criminal Justice Act, 2015
  • Relevant court judgments and official agency guidance

Each scenario lists its specific sources with sections and verification dates.

Technology

  • Built with React, TypeScript, Vite, and Tailwind CSS
  • Progressive Web App — installable, works offline
  • Client-side search — no server, no tracking
  • Hosted on Cloudflare Pages (static hosting)
  • Open source — github.com/knowyourrights-ng
